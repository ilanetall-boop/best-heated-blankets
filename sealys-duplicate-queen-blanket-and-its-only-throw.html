What's identical

One name, one price bracket, one color — how to tell it's one blanket

Start with the title, because that's the fastest tell. Both queen listings run the exact same string: "Sealy Electric Blanket Queen Size Heated Dual Control Auto Shutoff 84x90." Not a similar title with a word swapped — the same words, in the same order, down to the size notation. When two listings from the same brand share a title this precisely, that's a stronger signal than price or photos that you're looking at one product catalogued twice, not two competing variants worth weighing against each other. We've seen brands run several genuinely different queen blankets under titles that vary by color or fabric — Eydna's three queen listings each name a distinct fabric and color in the title itself. Sealy's two queen listings name nothing that distinguishes them at all.

The size matches too: both state 84×90″, the standard queen footprint for this category, sized to cover a queen mattress with overhang on both sides rather than a full or king blanket relabeled. Neither listing's title mentions a color name. That's notable on its own, because Sealy's throw — the only other product in this comparison — does name its color directly, calling itself "Camel." Looking at both queen listings' own product photos, the blanket itself reads as the same warm tan/beige shade either way, close to what Sealy's throw calls Camel, even though neither queen title commits to that word.

The stated feature set matches as well: "Dual Control Auto Shutoff" appears identically in both titles, with no further detail in either one about how many heat levels that control system offers or how long the auto-off window runs before it cuts power. That absence of detail is itself consistent across both listings — neither one is more specific than the other, which is part of why we're confident these are the same blanket rather than one listing being a stripped-down version of the other.

Put those together — identical title, identical size, identical feature claim, the same color as far as the photos show — and the honest read is that B0B28VJGS2 and B0B28X1BSG are the same physical blanket, sold under two separate detail pages. Price is the one number that doesn't match exactly, and that's worth its own section rather than a footnote.

The part that doesn't match

What actually differs between the two queen listings

Three things separate B0B28VJGS2 from B0B28X1BSG, and none of them is a meaningful spec. The first is the ASIN itself — the identifier Amazon assigns per detail page, which is the whole reason two listings for one product can coexist at all. The second is price: $82.30 for B0B28VJGS2 versus $83.59 for B0B28X1BSG, a $1.29 gap that's small enough to read as ordinary Amazon price drift between two pages rather than a deliberate cheaper-versus-premium split the way Eydna's denim-blue queen blanket is ten dollars more for a genuinely different reason — fewer heat levels, stated outright in that title. Sealy's two listings don't state anything that would explain a price gap; $1.29 reads like the kind of difference that comes from two separate listings drifting slightly out of sync on Amazon's pricing engine, not from two different products.

The third difference is the one you'd actually notice if you opened both tabs side by side: the main product photo. B0B28VJGS2's listing photo shows the blanket folded flat into a stack, a smooth tan surface with nothing underneath visible. B0B28X1BSG's listing photo shows the same folded stack with one corner peeled back, revealing a quilted white liner stitched into what would be the underside or reverse face. That's a genuinely different image — but it's a photography choice, not evidence of a different blanket. A folded electric blanket has a visible top face and a hidden one regardless of which photo a given listing happens to use; one seller's photographer chose to show you the hidden face, the other didn't.

What we don't have, and won't guess at, is why Sealy or its sellers ended up running two ASINs for a title this identical. Nothing in either listing states a reason. We're also not going to assume one listing is "the real one" and the other a copy — from the data available, both are live, both are priced within a dollar and a quarter of each other, and both carry the identical core claim. If you've already added one to your cart, there's no confirmed spec reason to go hunting for the other.

The mechanism

Why a brand ends up with two ASINs for the same product

This isn't unique to Sealy, and it isn't evidence of anything shady on its own — duplicate ASINs for a single physical product are a known, mundane artifact of how Amazon's catalogue works, and they show up across brands and categories for a handful of recurring reasons.

The most common is a fulfillment-channel split: the same product gets listed once under Fulfilled by Amazon and again under a separate seller-fulfilled or distributor arrangement, because the two channels were set up as independent detail pages instead of being merged into one listing with multiple offers. Another is catalogue drift over time — a seller edits a listing's bullet points, images or backend keywords enough that Amazon's matching system treats the result as a sufficiently new product and spins up a fresh ASIN rather than updating the existing page in place. A third is multiple authorized resellers or regional distributors each generating their own detail page for the same UPC before Amazon's deduplication catches up, which doesn't always happen automatically or quickly. A fourth is more deliberate: a seller abandons an older ASIN after a suppressed listing or a run of returns, then relists an identical product fresh rather than trying to repair the original page's history.

We don't know which of these, if any, explains the specific Sealy case here — nothing in either listing states a reason, and we're not going to guess at internal catalogue decisions on the seller's behalf. What we can say is that the pattern itself — identical title, identical specs, nearly identical price, different ASIN — is exactly what each of those mechanisms produces, which is part of why we're confident this is a duplicate rather than two genuinely different blankets that happen to share a name.

Before you compare two tabs

The general rule for spotting a duplicate before you waste the comparison

Sealy's queen blanket isn't the first same-brand pair we've run into on this site, and it won't be the last. Before you treat two open tabs as two products worth weighing against each other, run through a short checklist: first, read the full, unabbreviated title of each listing side by side, not just the thumbnail or the first few words Amazon truncates in search results. If the titles match word for word, that's the strongest single signal you're looking at one product, not two — it's exactly what tipped us off here, the same way it was the first thing we checked against Eastsora's two heated throws, which turned out to differ in construction even with a near-identical heat-level claim on both boxes.

Second, check the stated dimensions and any named color, not just the photo Amazon happens to be showing you in search results — thumbnails get swapped and reused more often than titles do. Third, look at price: two listings from the same brand landing within a dollar or two of each other is a much stronger "same product" signal than two listings landing twenty or thirty dollars apart, which more often points to a genuine difference in materials or features, the way it does across Bedsure's three priciest queen blankets. Fourth, scroll the product page itself for a "Compare similar items" or "Other sellers on Amazon" module, which sometimes surfaces the sibling ASIN directly on the page rather than leaving you to find it through search.

If all of that lines up — matching title, matching size, matching price bracket, matching feature claim — the honest move is to stop treating the two tabs as a comparison and just pick whichever one is cheaper or already in your cart. Save the actual side-by-side research for listings that differ in something you can point to, the way the throw here genuinely differs from either queen by size, controller count and price, rather than for two pages describing the same blanket twice.
