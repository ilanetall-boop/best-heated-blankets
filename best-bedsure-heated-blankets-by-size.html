Bedsure's Heated Blankets at Every Size, Compared

Bedsure sells the same basic electric blanket in four sizes, from a $38.99 throw to a $76.47 dual-control queen. Because it is one brand across the whole run, the price difference between sizes is close to the cleanest read you will get on what size actually costs — without a second brand's markup or missing-spec listing muddying the comparison.

By Ilane Tall · Updated November 10, 2026 · Research-based.
Quick answer

Same spec sheet, four price tags

All four Bedsure sizes — Throw, Twin, Full, and Queen — run six heat settings and six timer presets up to ten hours, and all four carry the same Intertek-recognised safety testing claim. What actually changes as you move up is dimension, price, and two features that do not travel with every size: preheat, which is on the throw, twin, and full but missing from the queen listing, and dual control, which exists only on the queen. The jump from throw to twin costs about $14. Twin to full costs about $7. Full to queen is the only size where the controller itself changes, for roughly $16.48 more.

This guide works through what stays identical across the line, what each step up actually buys you, a pick for each size, and the one size where a different brand is worth a second look before you default to Bedsure out of habit. Jump straight to the four picks or to where Bedsure stops being the obvious default.

The method

Why one brand across four sizes is the cleanest comparison

Comparing heated-blanket sizes across different brands is a noisier exercise than it looks. A $40 throw from one maker and a $75 queen from another differ in size, yes, but they can also differ in fabric weight, controller type, wiring layout, and whatever safety testing each company chose to publish or not publish — and now you cannot tell how much of the price gap is size and how much is just brand. We ran into exactly that problem comparing dual-control queens from three different brands in our under-$60 queen roundup, where only one listing bothered to state its heat-setting count at all.

Bedsure's own catalogue removes most of that noise. The Bedsure Heated Blanket Throw Blanket with Preheat Function (50×60″), the Bedsure Electric Blanket Twin with Preheat Function (62×84″), the Bedsure Heated Blanket Full Size, and the Bedsure Heated Blanket Queen Size, Dual Control are all grey, all built around the same fast-heating flannel construction the brand uses across its line, and all priced within the same narrow window a single company sets for its own products. When four sizes share a brand, a fabric family, and a safety claim, the price difference between them stops being a brand tax and starts being closer to an honest answer to the question "what does size actually cost."

That is not a claim that Bedsure is the only brand worth buying, and we get to the point where it stops being the obvious pick further down. It is a claim that if you specifically want to isolate the cost of size — holding fabric, controller family, and testing claims roughly constant — a single brand's size run is the fairest dataset available, and Bedsure happens to sell one that spans throw through queen.

The constant

What stays identical across all four sizes

01

Six heat levels

Throw, Twin, Full, and Queen are all built around the same six-level heat range, so "level 4" means roughly the same thing on a sofa throw as it does on a shared bed.

02

Six timer settings

Every size offers six timer presets running up to ten hours. A sofa session and most of an overnight sleep are both covered by the same preset ladder, whichever size you buy.

03

Intertek-tested safety claims

Bedsure states its heated blankets are evaluated through an Intertek-recognised testing programme. The claim is consistent across the brand's line, not limited to one size or price point.

04

Grey, fast-heating flannel

All four ship in grey and lean on the same fast-heating flannel build Bedsure markets across its catalogue — the fabric experience does not change as the dimensions do.

The practical effect is that choosing a size with Bedsure is almost entirely a decision about dimensions and budget, not about hunting for a model with a better heat range or a longer timer hiding at a different price point. That is worth knowing before you compare it against a single-size blanket from another brand, like the reversible model in our flannel-and-sherpa throw comparison, where the second fabric side is the point of the purchase rather than the heat settings.

Step one

Twin to full: a jump that costs less than you'd expect

The Twin at $52.99 and the Full at $59.99 sit just $7 apart, which is the smallest gap anywhere in this four-size run. Both are built for a single mattress rather than a sofa, both carry the preheat function, and both run the same six-level heat range and six timer presets as the throw and the queen. What you are actually paying the extra $7 for is coverage: a full-size mattress is wider than a twin, and the Full blanket is cut to match that width so it does not leave cold strips down either side of the bed.

That makes the Twin-to-Full step the easiest upgrade decision in the whole line. If you already know the mattress is a full rather than a twin, there is essentially no reason to downsize to save $7 and end up with a blanket that does not reach the edges. The calculation only gets interesting once you cross into the Full-to-Queen step, where the price gap widens and a genuinely new feature shows up for the first time.

Step two

Full to queen: where dual control finally shows up

The jump from Full ($59.99) to Queen ($76.47) is $16.48 — more than double the Twin-to-Full gap — and for the first time in the line, the extra money is not only buying square footage. The Bedsure Heated Blanket Queen Size is explicitly sold as a Dual Control Electric Blanket, meaning two people sharing the bed each get their own zone and their own controller rather than negotiating over one shared dial. None of the smaller three sizes in this lineup offer that; a dual-zone controller only makes sense once two people are routinely sharing the blanket, which is a queen-bed problem more than a twin or full one.

This is also the point where it is worth checking what else is available at a similar price before defaulting to Bedsure. Our queen roundup under $60 covers three other dual-control queens that undercut Bedsure's $76.47, and if grey is not what you are after, our color-focused queen comparison looks at dual-control queens in purple, navy, and cream. The Bedsure queen is not a bad buy at $76.47 — it is simply the size where it stops being the default just because the smaller sizes were.

The feature that doesn't travel

Preheat on three sizes, and its absence on the queen

Three of the four listings name preheat directly: the Throw is titled "with Preheat Function," the Twin is titled "with Preheat Function," and the Full is titled "with Preheat." The Queen listing — "Bedsure Heated Blanket Queen Size, Dual Control Electric Blanket" — does not mention preheat at all. Preheat runs the blanket hot for a short stretch before settling into a lower level, which removes the step of manually dialing a cold blanket down once it has warmed up. It is a convenience feature, not a safety one, but it is the kind of detail that is easy to assume carries across an entire size line when it does not.

The likely trade reflects the controller swap: the queen's dual-zone design puts two independent controllers on one blanket instead of one controller running a single preheat routine, and Bedsure's listing does not carry the preheat claim over to that design. If a quick, hands-off warm-up before bed matters more to you than splitting the bed into two zones, that is a real reason to look at the Full at $59.99 over the Queen at $76.47 even if you have the mattress width for a queen — or to compare the Queen against a non-Bedsure dual-control option that does state a preheat function, which is exactly the kind of line-item check our FVERWAG listings comparison walks through for a different brand selling what looks like the same blanket three times.

The honest limit

The size where Bedsure stops being the obvious default

Up through the Full, Bedsure's size run is close to a no-brainer: the price steps are small ($14 then $7), every size keeps the same heat range, timer presets, preheat function, and safety claim, and there is no competing feature worth chasing elsewhere. That logic breaks at the Queen. A $16.48 jump is the largest single step in the line, it is the size where the preheat function disappears from the listing, and — more importantly — it is the size where other brands are competing hardest on the exact feature Bedsure is finally offering, dual control.

That combination is worth pausing on before you buy the Bedsure queen purely out of brand loyalty built up from a good experience with the throw or twin. Our queen comparison under $60 lines up three other dual-control queens, some priced below $76.47, and our non-grey queen guide is useful if color is also a factor Bedsure's single grey colorway does not address. None of that makes the Bedsure Queen a bad blanket — the heat range and timer presets are identical to the rest of the line, and the dual-zone split genuinely solves the one-controller argument. It simply means the Queen is the one size in this four-size run worth comparison-shopping before you buy, where the Throw, Twin, and Full mostly are not.

How we picked

One brand, every size it sells

Rather than compare Bedsure against a mix of competitors, we pulled every size Bedsure currently lists with a complete published specification — finished dimensions, heat-setting count, timer behavior, preheat status, and any stated safety certification — and lined them up in order: Throw, Twin, Full, Queen. Each had to carry the same brand-level claims (the six-and-six control scheme, the Intertek-recognised testing programme) before we would treat the comparison as a fair read on size alone rather than a mix of unrelated specs.

FAQ

Common questions

Do all four Bedsure sizes have the same heat settings?

Yes. The Throw, Twin, Full, and Queen all run the same six heat settings and the same six timer presets up to ten hours. What changes between sizes is the finished dimensions, the price, and two features that do not carry across every size: preheat, which the Throw, Twin, and Full all state and the Queen listing does not, and dual control, which only the Queen has.

Which Bedsure size is the best value?

The Full, by a clear margin. At $59.99 it costs only $7 more than the Twin at $52.99, but it is cut to actually cover a full-size mattress's width rather than leaving cold strips down the sides the way a twin blanket would on a wider bed. The jump from Twin to Full is the smallest price step anywhere in the line for a real coverage upgrade.

Why doesn't the Bedsure Queen have a preheat function?

The listing simply does not state one, unlike the Throw, Twin, and Full, which are all titled "with Preheat Function" or "with Preheat." The likely trade is the controller: the Queen is built around dual-zone control, with two independent controllers running two sides of the bed, rather than one controller running a single preheat routine. If preheat matters more to you than splitting the bed into two zones, the Full at $59.99 keeps that feature and the Queen does not.

Is the Bedsure Queen still worth buying at $76.47?

It can be, especially if you specifically want dual-zone control from a brand whose smaller sizes you already trust. But the Queen is also the one size in this line worth comparison shopping first: it is the largest price jump in the run, it drops the preheat feature the other three sizes share, and other brands compete directly on dual control at this size. Our queen comparisons under $60 and in colors other than grey are worth checking before buying this one by default.
